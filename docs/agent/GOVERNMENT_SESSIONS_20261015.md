## Approved rules

Further participation work is specified in [Government participation implementation](GOVERNMENT_PARTICIPATION_IMPLEMENTATION.md): non-ruler membership and proposals, political beliefs, sponsorship and action wording, territorial growth, and player-selected founding institutions. It is a design extension, not part of the deployment evidence below.

- Regular sessions meet on the displayed 15th and 30th of each Reign season. Reign has a 126-day year and 31.5-day seasons, anchored by `ReignCalendarService`; native day modulo arithmetic is not the calendar. Present ordinary business at session N and decide it at N+1. Each political party has one new proposal slot per session, plus one for the ruler; sessions can introduce several matters or none. Time-critical matters retain extraordinary hearings with the same constitutional weights.
- Crown shares by authority level 1 through 5 are 100%, 75%, 50%, 25%, 0%. Divide the rest equally among individual seated members. The largest weighted option wins, including a 45/35/20 result; there is no runoff or ordinary ruler override. Ties retain the existing status-quo preference and deterministic fallback for essential allocations. No valid ballots means no invented result. Native eligible fief recipients plus the ruler's clan remain on one ballot.
- Freeze authority and eligible seat shares when a matter is presented. A vacated seat abstains instead of increasing somebody else's share. New members participate in subsequently presented matters. Constitutional authority reductions retain their distinct consent/force rules; changing authority does not retroactively change a pending ballot.
- Public For, Against and Undecided positions identify real members and voting shares, not probabilities, guarantees or private relationship scores. Positions may change after private conversations. Lobbying, persuasion, bribes and deals belong in ordinary individual conversations, not the government session interface.
- At level 1, a matter has no effect without the ruler being present and agreeing. At levels 2–5, the government can decide while the ruler is absent. The existing Chancellor represents the Crown only while Active and available. A vacant/inactive/unavailable office abstains. Use the Chancellor's full established personality, relationships, memories and accepted conversations to choose; this is not a loyalty threshold. Publish only the chosen option's public label, never model prose derived from private memories. The ruler remains politically responsible for the Crown's choice. At level 5 a recommendation has no vote. A failed model request records abstention after bounded retries; a superseded reply cannot overwrite a newer ruler vote. NPC kingdoms obey the same presence rule without an invented Chancellor office.
- Opposing a lord causes a smaller relationship loss even if the lord ultimately wins, and a larger loss when the Crown helps defeat that lord's preference. Initial losses at levels 2–5 are respectively −4/−3/−2/−1 when the lord wins and −8/−6/−4/−2 when defeated. Include named petitioners, sponsors and affected lords, even without seats, and consolidate overlapping roles. Keep all relationship numbers private. Only an existing dated spymaster snapshot may reveal a relationship through its authorized intelligence view. Government Trust is retired.
- Native policy enactment/repeal remains integrated with Reign. Native buttons and competing native voting popups stay disabled for owned business; native succession retains its documented exception. Royal Privilege cannot recreate a Crown override. Native Senate/Magistrates policies do not replace cultural membership or appoint the Chancellor.

## Initial edict tuning

The eight candidates discussed with the user are included in the approved scope. Their numbers are initial balancing values, pending the later player test plan.

| Edict | Benefit | Cost / limit |
|---|---|---|
| Castle-Guard Service | Each functioning bound village supplies one existing basic garrison recruit per three days | Maximum 30 recruits issued per settlement per 42-day term; actual volunteer and recruitment payment, capacity, wages and food; −0.5 town loyalty/day, −5% bound-village production; withdrawals never replenish the issuance cap |
| Extraordinary Subsidy | +15% settlement taxes, with a named recipient | −0.5 loyalty/day; expires after two regular-session intervals; renewal requires a vote; no equivalent extraordinary-tax stacking |
| Garrison Billeting | −15% garrison wages | −0.5 town loyalty/day, −5% local production |
| Public Works Obligation | +15% construction in named settlements | −0.3 loyalty/day, −5% bound-village production; ends with project or deadline |
| Free Market Charter | +10% workshop production, +0.3 prosperity/day | −15% tariffs; conflicting monopoly/toll rights require an explicit replacement proposal |
| Public Granary Ordinance | Purchases/stores actual grain and releases it during shortages; +0.5 loyalty/day while distributing | Voted finite budget and stocks, storage cap, no unfunded effects |
| Harvest Exemption | +10% village production | −20% recruit replenishment, compulsory garrison levy suspended; temporary |
| Charter of Local Customs | +0.75 loyalty/day where owner and settlement cultures differ | −10% taxes, −10% recruit replenishment; no change to culture or voting power |

Each proposal must identify scope, beneficiaries, costs, funding, duration and conflicts before the vote. Existing native policies remain visible; do not silently replace their effects with invented values or duplicate bonuses.

A completed vote remains final if implementation fails for lack of funds or another execution problem. Preserve the ballots and failure record without automatically retallying or repeating an uncertain effect. Expired or repealed granary reserves return only to the original clan; unavailable recipients leave a visible pending return that retries without reactivating the edict or creating resources.

Historical inspiration: [castle guard and requisitions](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/featured-documents/magna-carta/translation.html), [tax consent](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/originsofparliament/birthofparliament/overview/edward/), [market charters](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/markets-fairs/), [castle labour](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/medieval-castles/), and [public grain provision](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1994-0915-352). These support the institutions, not the numerical game tuning.
